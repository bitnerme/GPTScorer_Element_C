Element H: Prototype Testing and Data Collection Plan

H1 – Addressing Requirements
Evaluate how completely the testing plan addresses the project’s high-priority design requirements.

5 = Nearly all or all major requirements are clearly tested
4 = Many major requirements are tested
3 = Some requirements are tested
2 = Only a few requirements are tested
1 = One requirement is minimally tested
0 = No meaningful testing evidence

Guidance:
- Requirements may be explicitly stated or inferred from testing content
- Requirements must connect to actual test methods
- Mentioning a requirement without testing it lowers the score
- Distinct tests for distinct requirements support higher scores


H2 – Conduct of Tests
Evaluate the effectiveness and clarity of the testing procedures.

5 = Procedures are highly detailed, logical, controlled, and replicable
4 = Procedures are generally detailed and effective
3 = Procedures are adequate but partially unclear or incomplete
2 = Procedures are vague or only partially effective
1 = Minimal procedure information
0 = No meaningful procedure description

Guidance:
- Strong procedures include setup, tools, measurements, variables, and sequence of actions
- Another person should be able to replicate the tests
- Vague phrases like “we will test it” lower scores


H3 – Quantity of Tests Planned
Evaluate whether enough testing is planned to evaluate the design requirements.

5 = Testing quantity could feasibly evaluate nearly all requirements
4 = Testing quantity could feasibly evaluate most requirements
3 = Testing quantity could feasibly evaluate some requirements
2 = Limited quantity of tests
1 = Minimal testing quantity
0 = No feasible testing plan

Guidance:
- Multiple trials are expected
- Seven or more trials per major test is preferred
- Missing trial counts or weak coverage caps the score at 2
- Testing should collectively address most important requirements


H4 – Logic of the Plan
Evaluate the logic and evaluation framework behind the testing plan.

5 = Thorough, logical, and well-developed explanation
4 = Logical and generally developed explanation
3 = Adequate explanation with partial logic
2 = Weak or partially logical explanation
1 = Minimal logic or explanation
0 = No explanation

Guidance:
- Strong responses include:
  - expected results or ranges
  - measurable outcomes
  - pass/fail criteria
  - thresholds or tolerances
  - goals beyond minimum passing
- Tests should clearly connect outcomes back to requirements
- Missing thresholds or success criteria caps the score at 2


H5 – Use of Field Experts
Evaluate whether field experts reviewed and influenced the testing plan.

5 = More than two experts gave feedback that was incorporated
4 = Two experts gave feedback that was incorporated
3 = One expert gave feedback that was incorporated
2 = One expert gave feedback only
1 = Expert mentioned but no documented response
0 = No expert involvement

Guidance:
- Feedback may be paraphrased
- Revisions based on expert feedback must be evident for scores above 2
- Named experts without meaningful interaction score low


Global Evaluation Philosophy

Score based on explicit, testable evidence rather than formatting quality.

Do:
- Infer likely requirements when clearly implied by testing content
- Accept functionally equivalent wording
- Reward coherent and replicable test logic

Do not:
- Award points for headings without substance
- Assume missing logic
- Credit vague mentions or unsupported claims

High scores require:
- clear requirement alignment
- replicable procedures
- sufficient trials
- measurable evaluation criteria
- evidence of expert-informed revision
