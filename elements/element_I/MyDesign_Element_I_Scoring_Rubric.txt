Element I: Testing, Data Collection, and Analysis

I1 – Importance of Tests
Evaluate whether the student conducted tests for important design requirements.

5 = Tests all high-priority requirements
4 = Tests most high-priority requirements
3 = Tests about half of the high-priority requirements
2 = Tests some low- and high-priority requirements
1 = Tests only low-priority requirements
0 = No meaningful testing of requirements

Guidance:
- Testing may be physical or mathematical modeling.
- Requirements may be explicitly stated or inferred from the testing discussion.
- Strong scores require evidence that important requirements were actually tested, not merely mentioned.


I2 – Comprehension of Testing Procedure
Evaluate the student’s understanding of testing procedure, including data gathering and analysis.

5 = Considerable understanding of testing procedure
4 = Ample understanding of testing procedure
3 = Adequate understanding of testing procedure
2 = Partial or overly general understanding
1 = Minimal understanding
0 = No meaningful understanding of testing procedure

Guidance:
- Strong responses explain how tests were conducted and how data was gathered.
- Evidence may include variables, controls, repeated trials, measurement methods, raw data, and analysis methods.
- If procedures are unclear but outcomes show intentional testing logic, partial credit may be appropriate.


I3 – Detail of Design Analysis
Evaluate the detail of the analysis explaining how effectively the design met stated goals.

5 = Consistently detailed analysis, generously supported by pictures, graphs, charts, or other visuals
4 = Generally detailed analysis, generally supported by visuals
3 = Somewhat detailed analysis, at least somewhat supported by visuals
2 = Partial analysis, partially complete or partially correct, minimally supported by visuals
1 = Attempted analysis with little or no visual/data support
0 = No analysis

Guidance:
- Strong responses summarize data, interpret results, and connect findings to design goals.
- Visuals may include graphs, charts, photos, tables, spreadsheets, or other displays of data.
- Strong narrative analysis may partially compensate for limited visuals if the design logic and trends are clear.


I4 – Summary of Data Implications
Evaluate whether the analysis summarizes what the data means for improving the design and solving the problem.

5 = Robust summary; implications address all major results and next steps
4 = Overall summary; implications address most or nearly all major results
3 = Overall summary; implications address at least some results
2 = Developing summary; implications address at least some results
1 = Partial and overly general summary; weak implications
0 = No meaningful summary or implications

Guidance:
- Strong responses explain what the data means for the design’s success or failure.
- Strong responses identify implications, design changes, or next steps based on evidence.
- Reflection and interpretation may raise the score when connected clearly to test results.


Global Scoring Guidance

Score based on evidence in the Element I document.

Do:
- Infer requirements when clearly implied by testing and analysis.
- Accept physical testing or mathematical modeling.
- Reward meaningful analysis, reflection, and interpretation.
- Give credit for logical testing outcomes even if formatting is imperfect.
- Consider raw data, charts, photos, tables, graphs, and written explanation.

Do not:
- Award high scores for headings or template structure alone.
- Assume testing occurred if only goals or plans are described.
- Credit vague claims without data, results, or analysis.
- Require perfect formatting if the testing logic and data interpretation are clear.

Element I is about completed testing, collected data, analysis of results, and implications for the design.
